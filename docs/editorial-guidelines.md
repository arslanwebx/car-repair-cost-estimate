# Carspect editorial guidelines

Future articles must solve one defined vehicle-owner problem, answer the main question near the beginning, and add original practical value. Authors and reviewers must be identified truthfully. Changing prices, safety guidance, law, insurance, and repair procedures require current reliable sources with facts clearly separated from estimates or opinion.

Every article needs visible publish and review dates, descriptive headings, short readable paragraphs, honest inspection guidance, and a natural link to the estimator. Add examples, calculations, diagrams, or original images when useful. Avoid generic introductions, keyword-driven filler, unsupported accuracy claims, duplicate city or model pages, and FAQs that merely repeat the article.

Planned topic clusters include repair costs; bumper, dent, scratch, paint, hail, and collision repairs; panels; OEM versus aftermarket parts; labor and paint; ADAS; insurance versus out-of-pocket decisions; total loss; vehicle and regional differences; damage photography; shop estimates; hidden damage and supplements; used-car inspections; and rental or rideshare damage.
